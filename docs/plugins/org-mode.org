#+TITLE: Org mode
#+DESCRIPTION: Build each Org mode file into a page, without Emacs, with the orgmode plugin.
#+OPTIONS: ^:{}

=orgmode=, one of [[file:using-plugins.md::#littles-own-plugins][little's own plugins]], makes each [[https://orgmode.org][Org mode]] file in =docs/=, =.org=, a [[file:using-plugins.md::#pages-in-other-formats][page]], without Emacs, and without exporting it first.
Name it in your site's [[file:using-plugins.md::#naming-a-plugin][=plugins=]]:

#+BEGIN_SRC yaml
# little.yml
plugins:
  orgmode: https://github.com/withlittle/little/releases/download/orgmode-v0.1.0/little_orgmode.wasm
#+END_SRC

It has no settings.
This page is one Org file, =docs/plugins/org-mode.org=.

* The keywords

The file's =#+TITLE:= is the page's title and its =#= heading, and its =#+DESCRIPTION:= is the page's description, wherever they are in the file.
A page can also start with [[file:../writing/frontmatter.md::#in-another-format][frontmatter]], above its keywords, which goes over them.
=#+AUTHOR:=, =#+DATE:=, and =#+EMAIL:= aren't shown, unless a macro, like ={{{author}}}=, shows them.

A macro that =#+MACRO:= defines, like =#+MACRO: kbd ~$1~=, is expanded, as are Org's =title=, =author=, =date=, =email=, =keyword=, and =results=.
=#+TODO:=, =#+SEQ_TODO:=, and =#+TYP_TODO:= set the file's TODO keywords.
Of =#+OPTIONS:=, only =^:= and =-:= are read, as [[*Text]] says.

* Text

Org's markup is Markdown's where Markdown has it: *bold*, /italic/, =verbatim= and ~code~, which are both code, and +struck through+.
Where it hasn't, it's HTML: _underlined_ is =<u>=, and the 2 in x^{2} and H_{2}O is =<sup>= and =<sub>=, or Markdown, where your site turns on the [[file:../writing/markdown.md::#choosing-markdowns-extensions][extensions]] =superscript= and =subscript=.

Like Org's export, the plugin reads =x^2= as a superscript, and the =_case= of =snake_case= as a subscript.
Set =#+OPTIONS: ^:{}=, as this page does, to read only those in braces, like =x^{2}=, or =^:nil= to read none.

=--= is an en dash, --, =---= an em dash, ---, and =...= an ellipsis, ..., unless the file sets =#+OPTIONS: -:nil=.
An entity, like =\alpha= or =\to=, is its character, \alpha or \to, and a line that ends in =\\= keeps its line break.

* Headlines and links

A headline, =* Title=, is a heading with one more =#= than it has =*=, from =##= to =######=, as the page's title is its =#=.
Its =CUSTOM_ID= property, or a target, =<<name>>=, in its title, leads to it too.
Its TODO keyword, priority, and tags are left out, as are its planning lines, like =SCHEDULED:=, and its drawers, but for a block's results.
A headline that starts with =COMMENT=, or is tagged =:noexport:=, is left out, with all that's under it.

A URL, like https://orgmode.org, is a link, as are =[[https://example.com][text]]=, =[[file:file.pdf][text]]=, and =<mailto:you@example.com>=.

A link to a headline, =[[*The keywords]]=, or to its =CUSTOM_ID=, =[[#id]]=, leads to it, and shows its title, as [[*The keywords]] does.
One to a target, =[[name]]=, leads to the target, =<<name>>=, or the table or block whose =#+NAME:= it is, or else to the headline of its title.
One to another page's file, like =[[./guide.org]]= or =[[file:../writing/markdown.md]]=, leads to that page, and =[[file:guide.org::#install]]= to the id =install= on it.
Any other search in a file, like =::*Install=, leads only to its page, and is warned of.
A link into Emacs, like =elisp:= or =info:=, keeps its text, and is warned of.

* Lists

Each of Org's lists is the Markdown list nearest it:

- Bullets :: Items that start with =-= or =+=, or =*= indented, nested as they're indented, are a bulleted list.
- Numbers :: Items that start with =1.= or =1)= are a numbered list. It starts at 1, or where its first item's counter, like =[@4]=, says.
- Checkboxes :: =[X]= and =[ ]=, after an item's bullet, are GitHub's task lists. =[-]=, for one partly done, is =[ ]=.
- Terms :: A term and what it means, like these, are a description list.

* Code

A block of code, =#+BEGIN_SRC lang=, is a code block, highlighted in its language.
An example block, =#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE=, or a run of lines that start with =:= and a space, is one with no language, and an inline block, like =src_python{print(1)}=, is code too.
A code block goes to [[file:using-plugins.md::#code-blocks][plugins]] that render its language, and a =mermaid= block is a [[file:../writing/diagrams.md][diagram]].

A block shows what its =:exports= header says: =code=, the default, =results=, =both=, or =none=.
Its results are those saved with it, under =#+RESULTS:=, as this one's are:

#+BEGIN_SRC python :exports both
print("Hello from Org mode")
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
: Hello from Org mode

=#+PROPERTY: header-args :exports both= sets =:exports= for every block in the file, and a headline's =header-args= property, for the blocks under it.

* Admonitions

#+BEGIN_NOTE
A special block named for one of GitHub's five [[file:../writing/markdown.md::#callouts][alerts]] is that alert, like this note.
Those are =NOTE=, =TIP=, =IMPORTANT=, =WARNING=, and =CAUTION=, and =HINT= is a tip too, and =DANGER= or =ERROR= a caution.
#+END_NOTE

An admonition can hold lists and code too.

* Other blocks

#+CAPTION: What other blocks are
| Block                                              | In the page                                                       |
|----------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Quote, =#+BEGIN_QUOTE=                             | A quote                                                           |
| Verse, =#+BEGIN_VERSE=                             | A paragraph for each stanza, with its line breaks and indentation |
| Center, =#+BEGIN_CENTER=, and other special blocks | What they hold                                                    |
| Export, =#+BEGIN_EXPORT html=, and =#+HTML:=       | Its HTML, as it is                                                |
| Export to another format, like =latex=             | Nothing                                                           |
| Horizontal rule, =-----=                           | A rule                                                            |
| Comment, =# text=, or =#+BEGIN_COMMENT=            | Nothing                                                           |

A caption, =#+CAPTION:=, is a paragraph in italics above its table or block of code, as this table's is.
An image's caption is below it.

* Tables

A table is one of GitHub's tables, with its header row, above its first rule, =|---+---|=, if it has one.
Each column is aligned as its cookie, =<l>=, =<c>=, or =<r>=, says, or else to the right if at least half its cells are numbers:

| Headline   | Heading     | Level |
|------------+-------------+-------|
| =* Title=  | =## Title=  |     2 |
| =** Title= | =### Title= |     3 |

A row of cookies is left out, as are the special column and rows that Org's spreadsheet reads.

* Images

A link to an image, like =[[file:diagram.png]]=, with no description, is the image.
Its alt text is its file's name, or the =:alt= of an =#+ATTR_HTML:= above it.
An image as a link's description, like =[[https://example.com][file:diagram.png]]=, is an image that's a link.

* Footnotes and their definitions

A footnote, =[fn:name]=, is numbered through the page, like this one.[fn:1]
Its definition, a line that starts with =[fn:name]=, can be anywhere in the file, and an inline one, =[fn::Its text.]=, is defined where it is.
A headline named =Footnotes=, for definitions at the file's end, is left out, with all that's under it but those definitions.

* Math

Math is TeX, written as [[file:../writing/math.md::#choosing-how-math-is-written][=markdown.math=]] says: =$…$= or =\(…\)= in a line, like $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$, or, on its own, =$$…$$=, =\[…\]=, or a LaTeX environment, like =\begin{equation}=.
A dollar sign that Org wouldn't read as math, like those in $5 and $10, stays a dollar sign.

* What's different

The plugin reads Org as Org's HTML export does, and leaves out what that leaves out, like comments, except that:

- It runs no code. A =#+CALL:= line, or an inline =call_name()=, is left out, and warned of.
- An include, =#+INCLUDE:=, isn't followed. It's left out, and warned of, and a setup file, =#+SETUPFILE:=, isn't read.
- A macro it doesn't expand, like ={{{time(%Y)}}}=, or one that runs code, with =(eval=, is kept as text, and warned of.
